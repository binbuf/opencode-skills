## Bottom line

## Key findings

Summarize the findings that most strongly affect the answer.

Separate strong findings from uncertain findings.

## Analysis

Develop the evidence and reasoning needed to support the answer.

Organize this section around the actual problem rather than around the order in which research was performed.

Use tables when they make comparisons materially clearer.

When comparing alternatives, keep criteria consistent.

## Evidence

Present the strongest supporting evidence.

Distinguish:

- documented fact
- measured result
- reported experience
- inference
- judgment

## Conflicting evidence and uncertainty

Explain important disagreements, unresolved questions, weak evidence, or information likely to change.

Do not manufacture false balance when one side has substantially stronger evidence.

## Practical implications

Explain what the findings mean for the user's actual objective.

When appropriate include:

- recommended approach
- architecture
- implementation implications
- risks
- tradeoffs
- things to test
- decisions that can safely be made now
- decisions that should wait for more evidence
